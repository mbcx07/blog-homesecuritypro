The Unseen Entry Points: How to Secure Every Access Point in Your Home

2026-07-28 · Security Systems · Tags: homesecuritypro, security systems

When most homeowners think about securing their property, they focus on the front door and maybe a few ground-floor windows. But the reality is that a determined intruder will look for the path of least resistance—often through entry points you never even considered. From pet doors and mail slots to second-story windows and garage service doors, these overlooked vulnerabilities can undermine even the most sophisticated security system. As a home security expert, I've seen countless homes where a single unguarded access point rendered an entire alarm setup useless. In this article, we'll explore the hidden entry points that burglars exploit, and how you can integrate smart locks, cameras, and sensors to create a truly comprehensive defense.

The Obvious and Not-So-Obvious Entry Points

It's easy to secure the front door with a deadbolt and a smart lock, but what about the side door that leads to the garage? Or the basement window that's partially hidden by shrubs? Burglars are opportunistic; they'll test every possible way in. According to the FBI, 34% of burglars enter through the front door, 22% through a first-floor window, and 22% through the back door. But that leaves a significant percentage using less conventional routes—garage doors, pet doors, and even second-story windows accessible via a tree or a ladder.

To truly secure your home, you need to conduct a thorough audit of every potential entry point. Start by walking around your property and noting every door, window, and opening. Don't forget:

  • Pet doors (often large enough for a small adult to crawl through)
  • Mail slots and package drop boxes
  • Garage service doors and the main garage door itself
  • Basement windows and crawl space vents
  • Second-story windows that might be reachable from a balcony or a sturdy tree branch
  • Sliding glass doors, which are notoriously easy to lift off their tracks

Once you've identified these points, you can begin to fortify them. But hardware alone isn't enough—you need a layered approach that combines physical barriers, smart technology, and monitoring.

Smart Locks: The First Line of Defense

Smart locks have revolutionized home security by offering keyless entry, remote access, and integration with other smart devices. But their true value lies in how you deploy them across all entry doors—not just the front door. Many homeowners install a smart lock on the main entrance but leave the back door with a traditional deadbolt that can be easily picked or bypassed. A smart lock on every exterior door ensures that you can control access from anywhere, receive alerts when a door is opened, and even grant temporary codes to service providers.

When choosing smart locks, look for models with built-in alarms, tamper detection, and compatibility with your existing security ecosystem. For example, the August Wi-Fi Smart Lock offers geofencing that automatically locks the door when you leave, while the Schlage Encode Plus integrates with Apple HomeKit for seamless automation. But remember: a smart lock is only as secure as its installation. Ensure that the strike plate is reinforced with long screws, and consider adding a door sensor that triggers an alarm if the door is forced open.

For sliding glass doors, consider smart locks specifically designed for these frames, or use a combination of a security bar and a sensor. The key is to make every entry point as difficult to bypass as the front door.

Camera Placement: Seeing the Unseen

Cameras are your eyes on the property, but their effectiveness depends entirely on placement. Most homeowners install a camera at the front door and maybe one in the backyard, leaving the sides of the house and less obvious entry points unmonitored. To cover the unseen entry points, you need a camera strategy that leaves no blind spots.

Start by mapping out the perimeter of your home. Place cameras at all ground-level entry points, including side doors, basement windows, and garage service doors. For second-story windows, consider a camera with a wide field of view that can capture anyone approaching from a ladder or a tree. Modern cameras like the Arlo Pro 4 or the Ring Stick Up Cam offer battery-powered flexibility, so you can place them anywhere without worrying about wiring.

But placement isn't just about coverage—it's about deterrence. A visible camera can discourage a burglar from even attempting a break-in. However, hidden cameras can catch them in the act. A combination of both is ideal. For example, a prominent camera at the front door signals that the home is protected, while a discreet camera near a pet door can capture evidence if someone tries to exploit that vulnerability.

When reviewing camera footage, look for models with motion detection, night vision, and two-way audio. These features allow you to not only see but also speak to a potential intruder, which can be a powerful deterrent. And with cloud storage, you can access footage from anywhere, ensuring that you never miss a critical event.

Sensors and Alarms: The Silent Guardians

While cameras provide visual confirmation, sensors and alarms are the silent guardians that detect unauthorized entry before a camera even captures it. Door and window sensors are the most basic, but they are essential for every entry point—not just the main ones. A sensor on a basement window that triggers an alarm when the window is opened can be the difference between a thwarted break-in and a successful one.

Modern sensors go beyond simple open/close detection. Glass break sensors listen for the frequency of shattering glass, while motion sensors can detect movement in a room even if the intruder has already entered. For pet doors, consider a sensor that detects when the flap is lifted, or better yet, install a smart pet door that only opens for your pet's microchip. This prevents a burglar from simply reaching through the flap to unlock the door.

When integrating sensors into your security system, think about the zones. Each entry point should be its own zone, so you know exactly which door or window was breached. This allows you to respond appropriately—whether it's calling the police or simply checking the camera feed. Many systems, like the SimpliSafe or Ring Alarm, allow you to customize zones and set different arming modes. For example, you might want to arm all sensors at night but only the perimeter during the day when you're home.

Integrating Everything: The Power of a Unified System

The true power of modern home security lies in integration. When your smart locks, cameras, and sensors work together, they create a cohesive defense that is far greater than the sum of its parts. Imagine this scenario: a sensor on your garage service door triggers, the camera immediately starts recording, and your smart lock automatically deadbolts the main door—all while you receive a push notification on your phone. This level of automation is possible with platforms like Apple HomeKit, Google Home, or Amazon Alexa, which allow you to create routines that respond to specific triggers.

For example, you can set a routine that turns on all interior lights when a motion sensor is triggered after dark, or one that sends a video clip to your phone when a camera detects a person. Some systems even use AI to distinguish between a person and a stray animal, reducing false alarms. The key is to choose components that are compatible with each other. If you're starting from scratch, consider a comprehensive system like the Vivint Smart Home or the ADT Command, which offer pre-integrated packages. If you're building your own, stick to major brands that support open standards like Z-Wave or Zigbee.

Integration also extends to your daily habits. For instance, you can set your smart lock to automatically lock when you leave the house, and your cameras to arm when you're away. This ensures that even if you forget to secure a side door, the system has your back. The goal is to make security effortless, so that you don't have to think about it—it just works.

Safety Tips for a Comprehensive Approach

Beyond hardware and integration, there are several practical safety tips that can significantly enhance your home's security. First, always keep your property well-lit. Motion-activated floodlights around entry points can startle a would-be intruder and make them visible to cameras. Second, trim shrubs and trees near windows and doors to eliminate hiding spots. A burglar prefers to work in the shadows, so a clear line of sight from the street can be a powerful deterrent.

Third, don't forget about the garage. Many homeowners leave the garage door partially open or use a keypad that hasn't been changed in years. Upgrade to a smart garage door opener that sends alerts when the door is opened, and consider a sensor that detects if the door is left ajar. Fourth, secure your Wi-Fi network. Smart security devices rely on your internet connection, and a weak password can leave them vulnerable to hacking. Use a separate network for your smart devices and enable two-factor authentication on all accounts.

Finally, get to know your neighbors. A neighborhood watch program or simply a group chat can be an invaluable resource. If someone sees a suspicious person near your home, they can alert you immediately. And when you're away on vacation, have a neighbor collect your mail and park in your driveway to make it look like someone is home. These simple habits, combined with your smart security system, create a multi-layered defense that addresses both the physical and the human aspects of security.

Conclusion

Securing your home is not about buying the most expensive equipment—it's about understanding where your vulnerabilities lie and addressing them systematically. The unseen entry points—the pet door, the side window, the garage service door—are often the ones that burglars target because they know homeowners overlook them. By installing smart locks on every exterior door, placing cameras to cover all angles, and integrating sensors that trigger alarms and notifications, you can create a security ecosystem that leaves no gap unguarded. Remember, the goal is not just to react to a break-in, but to prevent it from happening in the first place. With the right combination of technology and vigilance, you can make your home a hard target—and enjoy the peace of mind that comes with knowing every entry point is protected.
