The Security Gap: Why Your Front Door Isn't the Only Entry Point

2026-06-27 · Security Systems · Tags: security systems, homesecuritypro, The

When homeowners think about securing their property, the front door often takes center stage. We invest in heavy-duty deadbolts, smart locks with fingerprint scanners, and video doorbells that alert us to every package delivery. But here's the uncomfortable truth: burglars rarely use the front door. In fact, statistics consistently show that most break-ins occur through first-floor windows, sliding glass doors, and even attached garages. The front door is the most visible and most fortified entry point, which makes it the least attractive to a savvy intruder. The real vulnerabilities lie in the areas we tend to overlook—the side gate, the basement window, the dog door, the second-story balcony that's accessible from a tree branch. If you've built your entire security strategy around a single entrance, you've left the rest of your home exposed. This article will walk you through the hidden entry points that demand your attention, and show you how to extend your security coverage to every corner of your property.

The Overlooked Entry Points: Windows, Garages, and Sliding Doors

Let's start with the most common entry point after the front door: first-floor windows. According to the FBI, approximately 23% of burglars enter through first-floor windows, and another 22% through the front door. That means windows are almost as popular as the front door, yet they rarely receive the same security investment. A standard double-hung window can often be jimmied open with a flathead screwdriver if the lock is flimsy. Even worse, many homeowners leave windows unlocked during the day, especially in warmer months. The solution isn't just to lock them—it's to reinforce the glass and the frame. Consider installing window sensors that trigger an alarm when the glass is broken or the window is opened unexpectedly. For ground-level windows, security film can hold shattered glass together, making it much harder to push through. And don't forget about basement windows—they're often hidden from view and provide a perfect entry point for someone who wants to avoid being seen.

Sliding glass doors are another notorious weak spot. They're common in many homes, especially those with patios or decks. The standard lock on a sliding door is a small metal latch that can be easily defeated with a simple kick or by lifting the door off its track. The best upgrade is a security bar or a pin that goes through the track to prevent the door from sliding. But even more effective is a smart lock designed specifically for sliding doors, which can be integrated into your home security system. These locks use a motorized bolt that engages with the frame, and they can be controlled remotely. If you have a sliding door, make sure it has a secondary lock—either a surface-mounted deadbolt or a foot-operated lock. Also, check the glass: if it's not tempered or laminated, consider replacing it with impact-resistant glass, which is much harder to break.

Camera Placement: Covering the Whole Perimeter

Once you've identified the entry points, the next step is to ensure your cameras are watching them. Many homeowners make the mistake of placing a single camera at the front door and calling it done. But a comprehensive camera system should cover every vulnerable angle—not just the front porch. When I conduct security assessments for clients, I recommend a three-tier approach: perimeter cameras, entry-point cameras, and interior cameras. Perimeter cameras, such as the Arlo Pro 5 or the Ring Spotlight Cam, should be mounted at corners of the house to capture wide views of the yard and any approaching intruder. Entry-point cameras should be positioned directly above or beside each door and window, with a clear line of sight. For windows, consider a camera with a wide-angle lens that can cover multiple windows in one frame.

One of the best cameras for this purpose is the Reolink Argus 4 Pro, which offers 4K resolution and color night vision—so you can see exactly what's happening even in low light. It's battery-powered, so you don't have to worry about wiring, and it has a built-in siren that can be triggered remotely. For sliding doors, the Wyze Cam v4 is a budget-friendly option that offers excellent image quality and person detection. But remember, cameras are only as effective as their placement. Avoid pointing them directly into the sun, and make sure they're high enough to be out of reach but low enough to capture faces. Also, consider using a camera with a wide dynamic range (WDR) to handle backlighting, which is common in doorways and patios.

Smart Locks for Every Door: Not Just the Front

Smart locks have revolutionized home security, but most homeowners only think about them for the front door. If you have a side door, a back door, or a garage entry, those are just as vulnerable—and they often have less robust locks. A smart lock on every exterior door gives you centralized control and monitoring. For example, the August Wi-Fi Smart Lock is a retrofit that works with your existing deadbolt, so you can upgrade without replacing the entire handle. It allows you to grant temporary access codes to guests, service workers, or dog walkers, and you'll get notifications every time the door is locked or unlocked. For a more integrated solution, the Schlage Encode Plus is a keypad deadbolt that supports Apple HomeKey, so you can unlock it with your iPhone or Apple Watch. It also has built-in Wi-Fi, so you don't need a separate hub.

But what about the garage? Many people forget that the garage door is a huge entry point—often larger than a front door and frequently left open or unlocked. If you have an attached garage, consider installing a smart garage door controller like the MyQ Smart Garage Hub. This device lets you open and close your garage door from your smartphone, and it sends alerts if the door is left open. You can also set it to close automatically after a certain time. For the door between the garage and the house, install a smart lock as well—preferably one with a keypad so you don't have to carry a key. The Yale Assure Lock 2 is a great option because it works with both Wi-Fi and Z-Wave, and it has a touchscreen keypad that's easy to use even in the dark.

The Role of Lighting and Landscaping in Deterrence

Security isn't just about technology—it's also about making your home look like a difficult target. Burglars are opportunistic; they choose homes that offer easy access and low risk of being seen. That's why outdoor lighting is one of the most effective deterrents you can install. A well-lit exterior eliminates the shadows that intruders rely on to hide. Motion-activated floodlights are ideal for entry points like the side yard or the back patio. Look for lights with a wide detection range and a high lumen output—at least 700 lumens. The Ring Floodlight Cam is a popular choice because it combines a bright light with a camera and a siren. But you don't have to spend a fortune; even a simple solar-powered motion light from brands like LEPOWER can make a difference.

Landscaping also plays a role. Overgrown shrubs and trees provide cover for burglars to work unnoticed. Keep bushes trimmed below window level, and avoid planting tall hedges near entry points. Instead, consider planting thorny plants like roses or holly beneath windows—they're a natural deterrent. Also, make sure your house number is clearly visible from the street so emergency responders can find your home quickly. And don't forget about your fence: a solid privacy fence can actually work against you because it hides the intruder from neighbors. A fence with gaps or a see-through design (like a picket fence) allows natural surveillance from the street. If you have a solid fence, consider installing a security camera at the gate to monitor who comes and goes.

Building a Comprehensive Security Plan

Now that you've identified the gaps, it's time to bring everything together into a cohesive security plan. The most effective approach is to think in layers—what security professionals call defense in depth. The first layer is physical barriers: strong locks, reinforced doors, and window security film. The second layer is detection: cameras, motion sensors, and glass-break detectors. The third layer is response: an alarm system that alerts you and possibly a monitoring service. And the fourth layer is environmental design: lighting, landscaping, and even your daily habits. For example, always close and lock your garage door, even when you're home. Keep your windows locked, and use a dowel or a track lock for sliding doors. And make it a habit to check your security app before you go to bed to ensure all doors and windows are secured.

When choosing a security system, look for one that supports all these layers. Companies like SimpliSafe and Ring offer starter kits that include entry sensors, motion detectors, and a base station with a loud siren. But you can also build a custom system with individual components from different brands, as long as they integrate with a central hub like SmartThings or Hubitat. For example, you might use a Schlage smart lock, a Ring camera, and a Philips Hue motion sensor—all connected through your smart home platform. This gives you the flexibility to address your specific vulnerabilities. And don't forget about environmental sensors—smoke, carbon monoxide, and water leak detectors. These protect against threats that aren't criminal but can be just as devastating. Many security systems now offer these as add-ons, and they can be lifesavers.

Conclusion

Securing your home isn't just about the front door—it's about every potential entry point, every dark corner, and every unlocked window. By taking a holistic approach, you can close the security gaps that most homeowners overlook. Start by auditing your property: walk around your home and look for weak points. Check the locks on all doors, the integrity of your windows, and the accessibility of your garage. Then, invest in the right combination of cameras, smart locks, and lighting to cover those vulnerabilities. Remember, a burglar is looking for the path of least resistance. If your home is well-lit, well-monitored, and well-fortified at every entry point, they'll move on to an easier target. Your goal is to make your home a fortress—not just at the front door, but all the way around. With the right plan, you can achieve peace of mind knowing that every inch of your property is protected.
