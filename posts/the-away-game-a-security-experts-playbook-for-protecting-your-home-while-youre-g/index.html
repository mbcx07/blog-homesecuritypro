The Away Game: A Security Expert's Playbook for Protecting Your Home While You're Gone

2026-06-26 · Safety · Tags: homesecuritypro, The, safety

There's a moment every homeowner knows too well: you're sitting on a plane, or perhaps just pulling out of the driveway, and a nagging thought creeps in—did I lock the back door? Is the garage secure? What if someone notices the mail piling up? The reality is that an unoccupied home is a beacon for opportunistic criminals. Statistics consistently show that burglaries spike during vacation seasons and long work trips, precisely because an empty house signals vulnerability. But here's the good news: with the right combination of technology, planning, and old-fashioned common sense, you can turn your absence from a liability into a fortress. As a home security expert, I've spent years testing systems, reviewing cameras, and installing smart locks. In this guide, I'll share the strategies that actually work—not just the gadgets, but the mindset—so you can leave with confidence and return to a home that's exactly as you left it: safe, sound, and secure.

The Unoccupied Home: Understanding the Risks and the Mindset of a Burglar

To defend your home effectively, you must think like the person trying to break in. Burglars are not masterminds; they are opportunists who look for three things: ease of access, low visibility, and a lack of deterrents. An empty house checks all those boxes. When you're away, the usual deterrents—lights turning on, a dog barking, a car in the driveway—disappear. That's why the first step in any away-game strategy is to eliminate the obvious signs of vacancy. This goes beyond simply locking doors. It means creating the illusion of life. Smart plugs that turn lamps on and off at random intervals, a television that clicks on in the evening, and even a robotic vacuum that runs on a schedule can all make your home appear occupied. But technology alone isn't enough. You also need to consider the physical vulnerabilities: are your windows reinforced? Is your sliding glass door secured with a dowel or a secondary lock? Do you have a habit of leaving a spare key under the mat? These are the small details that a burglar will notice. In my experience, the most effective defense is a layered one—combining smart devices with physical barriers and behavioral changes. When you're away, every layer matters.

The Smart Lock Foundation: Keyless Entry and Remote Control

Smart locks are the cornerstone of any modern away-game plan. They offer something traditional deadbolts cannot: remote access and real-time monitoring. Imagine you're halfway to the airport and you realize you never locked the front door. With a smart lock, you simply open the app on your phone and lock it from anywhere. That alone is worth the investment. But the benefits go deeper. Many smart locks, like the August Wi-Fi Smart Lock or the Yale Assure Lock, integrate with your home security system, sending you alerts when the door is opened or closed. You can also create temporary access codes for house sitters, dog walkers, or repair technicians—and revoke them the moment they're no longer needed. This eliminates the risk of a copied key falling into the wrong hands. When choosing a smart lock for your away-game strategy, look for three features: Wi-Fi connectivity (so you don't need a separate hub), a keypad or fingerprint reader (so you don't have to hide a physical key), and a battery backup (so you're never locked out). I've tested dozens of models, and the best ones also offer geofencing—they automatically lock the door when your phone leaves a certain radius. That's a game-changer for forgetful travelers. But remember: a smart lock is only as secure as its installation. Make sure your strike plate is reinforced with long screws, and consider a smart lock with a built-in alarm that sounds if someone tries to tamper with it.

Camera Coverage That Travels with You: Reviews and Placement Strategies

When you're away, your security cameras become your eyes and ears. But not all cameras are created equal, and placement is just as important as the hardware. For an unoccupied home, I recommend a mix of indoor and outdoor cameras. Outdoor cameras should cover all entry points—front door, back door, garage, and any ground-floor windows. Look for models with high resolution (at least 1080p, but 2K or 4K is better), night vision, and motion detection with customizable zones. The Google Nest Cam (battery) is a solid choice for its ease of use and integration with Google Home, while the Arlo Pro 4 offers excellent video quality and a wire-free design. For indoor coverage, a camera like the Wyze Cam v3 provides budget-friendly peace of mind, but be mindful of privacy—place it in common areas, not bedrooms. The real power of cameras when you're away is the ability to check in live. You can see if a package was delivered, if a window was left open, or if someone is lurking. But here's a pro tip: don't just rely on live feeds. Set up motion-triggered alerts so you're notified the moment something happens. And consider a camera with two-way audio—you can speak through it to scare off a potential intruder or to greet a delivery person. Placement is critical. Mount outdoor cameras high enough to be out of reach (at least 8 feet) but low enough to capture faces. Angle them to avoid blind spots, and make sure they're not easily disabled. A camera that's visible is a deterrent; a camera that's hidden is a witness. For the away game, you want both.

The Sensor Web: Beyond Cameras and Locks

Cameras and locks are the stars, but the supporting cast—sensors—often makes the difference between a thwarted break-in and a successful one. Door and window sensors are the most basic, but they're essential. When you're away, you want to know if a window is opened, even if the camera doesn't catch it. Many systems, like the Ring Alarm or SimpliSafe, offer entry sensors that trigger a loud siren and send an alert to your phone. But don't stop there. Glass-break sensors detect the sound of shattering glass, which is invaluable if a burglar tries to smash a window. Motion sensors inside the home can be set to "away" mode, so if someone moves inside while you're gone, you'll know immediately. And for a truly comprehensive approach, consider environmental sensors—smoke, carbon monoxide, and water leak detectors. These protect against non-criminal threats that can cause just as much damage. When you're away, a burst pipe or an electrical fire can go unnoticed for days. Smart sensors can shut off your water main or alert the fire department automatically. In my experience, the best security systems are those that integrate all these sensors into a single app, so you can arm and disarm everything with one tap. Look for systems that offer cellular backup—if your Wi-Fi goes down, you'll still get alerts. And don't forget the simple things: a smart doorbell with a camera is a must-have, as it covers your front porch and lets you see who's there even when you're miles away.

The Human Element: Neighbors, House Sitters, and the Power of Community

No amount of technology can replace the value of a trusted neighbor. When you're away, your neighbors are your first line of defense. They can spot a suspicious car, pick up your mail, and even park in your driveway to make it look like someone's home. But you need to prepare them. Let your neighbors know you'll be away, and give them a way to reach you. If you have a smart lock, you can give them a temporary access code so they can water your plants or bring in packages. But be careful—only share codes with people you trust implicitly. A better approach is to use a house sitter, especially for longer trips. A house sitter not only keeps your home occupied but also cares for your pets and plants. If you can't find a sitter, consider a professional home watch service. These services check your property regularly, report any issues, and can even coordinate with your security system. The human element also extends to your own habits. Don't announce your vacation on social media until you're back. Avoid posting real-time updates that reveal you're away. And when you're packing, don't leave empty boxes on the curb—that's a clear signal to thieves. Instead, break down boxes and dispose of them discreetly. Finally, consider a neighborhood watch program. When neighbors look out for each other, the entire community becomes more secure. In my years as a security expert, I've seen countless cases where a simple neighborly check-in prevented a burglary. Technology is powerful, but it works best when combined with human vigilance.

Conclusion: The Away Game Is Won Before You Leave

Securing your home while you're away isn't about buying the most expensive gadgets—it's about creating a comprehensive strategy that addresses every vulnerability. Start with the basics: reinforce your doors and windows, install a smart lock, and set up a camera system that gives you eyes on your property. Add sensors to detect entry, motion, and environmental hazards. And never underestimate the power of your community—neighbors, house sitters, and local watch programs are invaluable allies. The key is to plan ahead. Test your system before you leave, make sure all batteries are charged, and set up alerts so you're notified of any activity. When you're on vacation, you should be relaxing, not worrying. With the right preparation, you can enjoy your time away knowing that your home is protected. Remember, the away game is won before you ever step out the door. So take the time to implement these strategies, and you'll return to a home that's safe, secure, and exactly as you left it—ready for your next adventure.
