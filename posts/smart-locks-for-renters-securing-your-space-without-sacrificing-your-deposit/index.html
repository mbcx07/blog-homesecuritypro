Smart Locks for Renters: Securing Your Space Without Sacrificing Your Deposit

2026-06-07 · Smart Locks · Tags: Smart, smart locks, homesecuritypro

Renting a home often means living with a landlord’s choice of locks, which are frequently outdated, easily compromised, or simply not your own. For many renters, the desire to upgrade to a smart lock is strong—but the fear of losing a security deposit or violating a lease can be paralyzing. The good news is that modern smart lock technology has evolved to offer flexible, non-destructive solutions that let you enjoy keyless convenience, remote access, and enhanced security without drilling a single hole or permanently altering your door. In this guide, I’ll walk you through the best smart lock options for renters, how to integrate them with cameras and security systems, and essential safety tips to keep your rental both secure and deposit-friendly.

The Renter's Security Dilemma

As a home security expert, I’ve seen countless renters struggle with the same problem: they want the peace of mind that comes with a smart lock, but they’re constrained by lease agreements and the practical reality of moving out someday. Traditional smart locks often require replacing the entire deadbolt, which means unscrewing the existing hardware and installing a new one. While this is reversible, it can leave marks, require filling screw holes, and sometimes even void a lease if the landlord has strict rules about modifications.

Moreover, many renters live in apartments or condos where the door is part of a common area, and the management may not allow any changes to the locking mechanism. This is where the latest generation of smart locks shines. They are designed with renters in mind, offering installation that takes minutes and leaves no trace when you move out. But before we dive into specific products, let’s understand the core challenge: balancing security with portability.

The key is to look for smart locks that either replace the existing deadbolt with a unit that uses the same screw holes (which is often acceptable if you keep the original hardware) or, better yet, attach to the existing deadbolt without any modification. The latter category includes smart locks that fit over your current thumb turn, like the August Smart Lock, or those that use adhesive mounts. These are truly renter-friendly because they don’t touch the door or the frame.

Smart Lock Options That Won't Damage Your Door

When you’re renting, the ideal smart lock falls into one of three categories: retrofit adapters, portable keypads, and temporary deadbolts. Let’s break them down.

Retrofit Adapters – These are devices that attach to the interior side of your existing deadbolt. The most popular example is the August Smart Lock (now in its 4th generation). It simply replaces the thumb turn on the inside of your door, using the existing screws. You don’t touch the exterior lock or the strike plate. Installation takes about 10 minutes with a screwdriver, and when you move out, you just put the original thumb turn back. The August Smart Lock connects to your Wi-Fi via a separate bridge (or uses built-in Wi-Fi in newer models) and allows you to lock/unlock via app, voice assistants, and even auto-unlock based on your phone’s location. It’s a brilliant solution for renters because it’s completely reversible and doesn’t alter the door’s appearance from the outside.

Portable Keypads – Another option is a standalone keypad that mounts on the door with strong adhesive, like the Wyze Lock or the Yale Smart Lock with a retrofit kit. However, some keypads are designed to be used with a separate smart deadbolt. For renters, the best approach is to use a keypad that communicates with a smart lock you already have, or one that is battery-powered and sticks to the door without screws. For example, the Schlage Encode Plus is a full smart deadbolt, but it requires replacing the entire lock. Instead, consider the August Wi-Fi Smart Lock with a separate keypad that you can mount with adhesive. The keypad allows you to enter a code to lock/unlock, and it can be removed without leaving marks.

Temporary Deadbolts – Some companies now offer smart deadbolts that are designed for renters, such as the Lockly Secure Pro with a mounting plate that uses the existing holes. But if you’re not allowed to change the deadbolt at all, you might look at a smart lock that fits over the entire interior assembly, like the Level Lock (which is a smart deadbolt that replaces the entire lock but uses the same holes). The Level Lock is actually a full replacement, but it’s designed to be invisible and uses the same screw holes, so it’s often acceptable if you keep the original. However, for a truly non-invasive option, the August is the gold standard.

When choosing a smart lock for a rental, always check with your landlord first. Many are open to smart locks as long as you restore the original hardware upon move-out. If you get written permission, you can even install a full smart deadbolt like the Yale Assure Lock 2 or the Schlage Encode Plus, which offer superior security and integration. But if you want to avoid any hassle, stick with the retrofit adapters.

Integrating Smart Locks with Cameras and Security Systems

A smart lock is just one piece of a comprehensive home security strategy. For renters, the ability to integrate your lock with cameras and a security system can dramatically enhance your safety without permanent installations. Most smart locks work with popular security ecosystems like Ring, SimpliSafe, and Abode. For example, the August Smart Lock integrates with Ring Alarm, allowing you to arm/disarm your system when you lock or unlock the door. This is a huge convenience and ensures that your security system is always armed when you’re away.

When it comes to cameras, you have two main options: indoor cameras and doorbell cameras. For renters, a video doorbell is often the best choice because it replaces your existing doorbell (which is usually a simple low-voltage device) and can be installed with just two screws. The Ring Video Doorbell and the Nest Doorbell are popular choices. They connect to your Wi-Fi and provide live video, two-way audio, and motion alerts. When paired with a smart lock, you can see who’s at the door and then unlock it remotely for a trusted visitor, all from the same app.

If you can’t install a doorbell, consider a standalone security camera like the Wyze Cam v3 or the Arlo Essential. These can be placed on a shelf or mounted with adhesive strips, and they offer excellent video quality and motion detection. You can set up automations: for example, when your smart lock is unlocked, the camera starts recording, or when you lock the door, the camera arms. This integration creates a layered security system that doesn’t require any drilling.

In terms of security systems, renters often shy away from traditional systems because they require sensors on doors and windows, which may involve drilling. However, many modern systems offer wireless sensors that use adhesive tape. SimpliSafe, for instance, has sensors that stick to your door and window frames, and they can be removed without leaving residue. The system also integrates with smart locks from August and Yale. So you can have a full security system with entry sensors, motion detectors, and a smart lock, all without permanent modifications.

When comparing security systems for renters, consider the following: SimpliSafe offers a no-contract, DIY setup with excellent customer support. Ring Alarm is affordable and integrates seamlessly with Ring cameras and smart locks. Abode is more customizable and works with a wide range of smart home devices. Each has its pros and cons, but for renters, the key is to choose a system that uses wireless, adhesive sensors and offers smart lock integration.

Top Smart Lock Picks for Renters

Based on my experience and testing, here are the best smart locks for renters, each with its own strengths.

  • August Wi-Fi Smart Lock (4th Gen) – This is my top pick for renters. It retrofits onto your existing deadbolt, installs in minutes, and works with Alexa, Google Assistant, and Apple HomeKit. The auto-unlock feature uses geofencing to unlock the door as you approach, and you can grant virtual keys to guests. The battery life is about 3-6 months, and it sends alerts when the door is left ajar. The only downside is that it doesn’t have a built-in keypad, but you can add the August Keypad for an extra cost.
  • Yale Assure Lock 2 with Wi-Fi – If you have permission to replace the deadbolt, this is a fantastic option. It offers a touchscreen keypad, a physical key override, and built-in Wi-Fi. It works with Apple HomeKit, Alexa, and Google Assistant. The installation is straightforward, and it uses the same screw holes as most standard deadbolts. However, it does require replacing the entire lock, so it’s not as renter-friendly as the August.
  • Level Lock (with Level Bolt) – The Level Lock is a smart deadbolt that fits inside your existing deadbolt, making it invisible from the outside. It’s a bit more complex to install, but it’s a great option if you want a sleek look. The Level Bolt is a retrofit kit that works with your existing deadbolt, similar to August but with a more discreet design. Both are good, but the Level Bolt is easier for renters.
  • Wyze Lock – This is a budget-friendly option that also retrofits onto your existing deadbolt. It includes a keypad that mounts with adhesive, and it works with the Wyze app. It’s not as polished as the August, but it’s very affordable and offers good basic features. The downside is that it doesn’t have as many integrations, but it does work with Wyze cameras.

When choosing a smart lock, consider the following factors: compatibility with your door type (some locks don’t work with mortise locks or certain deadbolts), battery life, connectivity (Wi-Fi vs. Z-Wave vs. Bluetooth), and whether you need a keypad. For renters, I always recommend the August because it’s the most flexible and least invasive.

Home Safety Tips for Renters Using Smart Locks

Even with a smart lock, you need to practice good security habits. Here are some essential tips to keep your rental safe.

First, always use a physical key as a backup. Smart locks can fail due to dead batteries or connectivity issues. Most smart locks come with a physical key override, but some (like the August) don’t have a keyhole on the outside. In that case, you need to have a backup plan, such as a hidden key or a keypad. If your smart lock doesn’t have a keypad, consider adding one or keeping a spare key in a secure location outside (though that’s not always safe).

Second, enable two-factor authentication on your smart lock app. This prevents unauthorized access to your lock’s settings. Also, use strong, unique passwords for your smart home accounts. Many people overlook this, but a compromised account can give a hacker control of your lock.

Third, regularly check the battery level of your smart lock. Most apps will alert you when the battery is low, but it’s a good habit to check monthly. Replace batteries promptly to avoid being locked out.

Fourth, be mindful of who you give access to. If you have a landlord or maintenance personnel, you can create temporary virtual keys that expire after a certain time. This way, you don’t have to share your main code. Also, revoke access for any previous roommates or guests.

Fifth, integrate your smart lock with your security cameras. Set up automations so that when the lock is unlocked, the camera starts recording, or when you lock the door, the camera arms. This creates a record of who enters and exits, which is invaluable in case of a break-in or dispute.

Finally, always lock your door, even when you’re home. Many people forget to lock their smart lock manually, but you can set up auto-lock to engage after a certain time. This is a great feature for renters who are often in a hurry.

Conclusion

Renting doesn’t mean you have to sacrifice security or convenience. With the right smart lock, you can enjoy keyless entry, remote access, and integration with cameras and security systems—all without damaging your door or losing your deposit. The August Smart Lock remains my top recommendation for renters due to its non-invasive installation and robust feature set. But regardless of which lock you choose, remember to pair it with a good security camera and a reliable alarm system to create a layered defense. By following the tips in this guide, you can turn your rental into a smart, secure sanctuary that you can leave without a trace. Stay safe, and happy locking!
